# Proof guide

Let $k=\overline{\mathbb Q}\subset\mathbb C$. The Lean endpoint and the exact unit predicate are given in [THEOREM.md](THEOREM.md).

## 1. Normalize the numerical relation

If $u$ and $u^{-1}$ are broad E-values, rescale their representing functions by the algebraic arguments to obtain broad E-functions $f,g$ with $f(1)=u$ and $g(1)=u^{-1}$. The relation $f(1)g(1)=1$ does not assert that $fg=1$ as functions.

## 2. Construct the Laurent system and its exact fiber

Broad arithmetic and a nonzero algebraic differential equation give a finite common system over $k[z,z^{-1}]$ containing $f,g$ as coordinates. For the algebra generated by its solutions, polynomial relations holding at one lift to functional relations with the prescribed specialization. Thus the kernel of numerical evaluation at one is exactly the ideal generated by $z-1$. In particular, the images of the two selected functions are inverse in the fiber.

The principal interfaces are `v5_actual_wide_common_laurent_system` and `v5WideSolution_exact_kernel`.

## 3. Descend the marked unit

A normalized formal fundamental system supplies the Picard–Vessiot comparison. Comparing it with the actual solution algebra transports the inverse pair to a unit and its inverse in the horizontal part of the mixed algebra. On the connected component, the two carry opposite characters. A pointed Kummer cover $z=t^q$ and a correcting unit allow the desired lift to descend while retaining the value at one. This step uses the character of the unit rather than a global trivialization of the whole connected torsor.

## 4. Preserve the differential equation

The mixed unit is horizontal, so the correcting unit supplies the entire logarithmic derivative of the descended unit. Compatibility with the cover gives
$$
t b'(t)=L(t)b(t),\qquad L\in k[t,t^{-1}].
$$
Because the realization is meromorphic at zero, write $b(t)=t^m a(t)$ there with $a(0)\ne0$. Then $L=m+t a'/a$, so $L$ has no negative powers and has constant coefficient $m$. Integrating $(L-m)/t$ with zero constant gives a polynomial over $k$. The normalized exponential times $t^m a(0)$ agrees with $b$ by the first-order equation; the leading Laurent coefficient $a(0)$ is algebraic. Evaluation at $t=1$ gives $u=\alpha\exp(\beta)$.

`v5ActualReciprocalExponential140` assembles the lift and the differential finish for the actual common system. No choice of cover, character, correcting unit, or first-order equation is an additional hypothesis of the final theorem.

## 5. Reverse inclusion

For algebraic $\alpha\ne0$ and $\beta$, the functions $\alpha\exp(\beta z)$ and $\alpha^{-1}\exp(-\beta z)$ are already strict E-functions. Both are therefore broad E-functions, and their values at one are reciprocal.
